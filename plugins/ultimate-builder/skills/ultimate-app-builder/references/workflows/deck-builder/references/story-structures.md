# Story structures

Pick one before writing slides. Each line is one slide (or a short run of
slides); the words in brackets are what its headline must say.

## Contents
- Pitch (investors)
- Sales / product demo
- Company introduction
- Report / review
- Keynote / talk
- Lesson / workshop
- Proposal / decision

## Pitch (investors) — 10–12 slides

1. Title: company + the promise in a few words
2. Problem [the pain, with a number]
3. Solution [what you do, in one picture]
4. How it works / demo [three steps]
5. Why now [what changed]
6. Market [who buys, how many, realistic size]
7. Traction [the best real number and its trend]
8. Business model [who pays, how much]
9. Competition [why you win — a 2 × 2 or a short table]
10. Team [why these people]
11. The ask [amount, what it buys, milestones it reaches]

## Sales / product demo — 8–12 slides

1. Title: the customer's outcome, not the product name
2. Their situation [what they deal with today]
3. The cost of it [time, money, risk — their numbers when possible]
4. What changes with the product [before → after]
5–7. Three capabilities, each as a real screen + the result it gives
8. Proof [a case study or result, real and attributed]
9. How you start [steps, time to value]
10. Pricing / options (when appropriate)
11. Next step [one clear action and date]

## Company introduction — 6–10 slides

1. Who we are [one-line positioning]
2. What we do [products / services]
3. Who we serve [customer types, real examples]
4. How we work [approach, principles — specific]
5. Results [real work, real numbers]
6. Team and locations
7. Contact / next step

## Report / review — 6–12 slides

1. Title with the period and the headline result
2. At a glance [3–4 key numbers vs. target]
3–8. One slide per finding: the claim as the headline, the chart as proof
9. What did not go to plan [honest]
10. What we will change [three actions]
11. Appendix (detailed tables) after the close

## Keynote / talk

Structure (after Nancy Duarte's "what is / what could be"): alternate
between today's reality and the better future, several times, ending on
the new bliss and a call to action. Big pictures, few words, one idea per
slide, a memorable moment (a demo, a story, a striking number) in the
middle.

## Lesson / workshop

1. Title and what they will be able to do by the end
2. Agenda with times
3. Why it matters (a real example)
4–n. Teach → show → try: concept slide, worked example, timed exercise
n+1. Check (quiz or recap questions)
n+2. Summary and where to go next

## Proposal / decision

1. The decision we need [stated plainly]
2. Recommendation [the option and why, in one line]
3. Context [what is happening]
4. Options compared [table: cost, time, risk, benefit]
5. The plan for the recommended option
6. Risks and how we handle them
7. The decision and the next step again

## Headline test

Read only the headlines, in order, out loud. If a listener could not
retell the argument, rewrite headlines before designing anything
(assertion–evidence: the headline is the claim, the slide body is the
evidence).
