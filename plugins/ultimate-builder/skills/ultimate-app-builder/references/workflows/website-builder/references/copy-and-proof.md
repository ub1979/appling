# Copy and proof

Words decide whether a site feels professional before the design does.
Write them before building, in `.sdlc/site-copy.md`, and get them approved
with the design direction.

## Headline

- Says what it is and the outcome for the visitor, in under ~10 words.
- Concrete beats clever: "Invoices paid in 3 days, not 30" over
  "Reimagine your finance workflow".
- Avoid: "revolutionary", "next-generation", "seamless", "unlock",
  "empower", "AI-powered" as the main claim, "Welcome to …".

## Subhead

One sentence: who it is for and how it works. 15–25 words.

## Calls to action

- One primary action across the whole site, with a verb and the result:
  "Start free trial", "Book a 20-minute demo", "Get the guide".
- A secondary action only when it serves a different visitor (e.g. "See
  docs" for developers).
- The same label every time it appears.

## Features and benefits

Lead with the result, then the mechanism:
"Never miss a renewal — we read every contract and remind you 30 days
before." Keep each to a heading plus ≤ 2 lines.

## Proof (truth rules)

Only what the owner can back up:

| Proof | Needs |
|---|---|
| Customer logos | Written permission or public use |
| Testimonials | A real person's words, name and role (photo if allowed) |
| Numbers | A source the owner can show |
| Press / awards | A link |
| Security / compliance badges | The certificate actually held |

When proof is missing, put a visible placeholder (`[Customer quote —
owner to supply]`) and list it in the report. Never fill the gap with
invented content, not even "for now".

## Tone

Match the audience: plain and warm for consumers and small businesses;
exact and calm for developers and enterprise. Short sentences, active
voice, second person ("you"). No exclamation marks in headlines.

## Microcopy

Button labels, form errors, empty and success states: hand to `ux-writer`
when the team has it; otherwise keep them specific ("Enter a work email",
not "Invalid input").
